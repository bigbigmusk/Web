Where to go

Western China Destinations

Six regions, each with its own landscapes, cultures and rhythm — from the Tibetan plateau to the Silk Road desert frontier.

Six regions

Choose Your Part of the West

Focus deeply on one region or link several into a single overland route. Each destination below works as a standalone journey or as part of a longer itinerary.

Tibet
Best for: culture & landscapes

Tibet

Lhasa's Potala Palace, the Jokhang Temple and the Barkhor pilgrim circuit, set against the wide light of the plateau. Travel out to Yamdrok Lake and the Himalayan frontier with permits and altitude planning arranged for you.

Explore Destination →
Xinjiang
Best for: Silk Road & deserts

Xinjiang

Kashgar's Old Town and Sunday bazaar, the Karakoram road to Karakul Lake, and the oasis vineyards of Turpan. A region of Uyghur culture, Tianshan peaks and the vast Taklamakan Desert.

Explore Destination →
Sichuan & Chengdu
Best for: food & easy starts

Sichuan & Chengdu

One of China's great food cities, with teahouses in People's Park and the giant panda research base nearby. Beyond Chengdu lie Mount Emei, the Leshan Giant Buddha and the Tibetan edge of western Sichuan.

Explore Destination →
Yunnan
Best for: villages & nature

Yunnan

Dali on the shore of Erhai Lake, the old town of Lijiang and the Tibetan highlands around Shangri-La. China's most ethnically diverse province, with gentle weather and deep village life.

Explore Destination →
Qinghai
Best for: lakes & plateau

Qinghai

Qinghai Lake, the largest in China, ringed by grasslands and summer rapeseed fields. From Xining you can reach Kumbum Monastery, the Chaka Salt Lake and some of the quietest plateau roads in the country.

Explore Destination →
Gansu & Silk Road
Best for: history & art

Gansu & Silk Road

The Hexi Corridor strings together the Mogao Caves at Dunhuang, the rainbow Danxia landforms of Zhangye and Jiayuguan, where the Great Wall meets the desert. The heart of China's Silk Road story.
