Yunnan Travel

Old towns, lakeside villages and terraced hills — gentle nature and the most diverse mix of cultures in China.

Overview

Yunnan at a Glance

Yunnan is the most ethnically diverse province in China, and travel here is built around its towns, villages and gentle landscapes. Most journeys begin in the capital, Kunming, before heading to Dali on the shore of Erhai Lake, the heartland of Bai culture, with its old town set between lake and mountains.

North lies Lijiang, whose Naxi old town of cobbled lanes and canals sits beneath the Jade Dragon Snow Mountain. From here the land rises toward Shangri-La, a Tibetan town on the edge of the plateau, by way of Tiger Leaping Gorge, one of the deepest river canyons in the region and a fine walk for those who want it.

To the south-east, the Yuanyang rice terraces — shaped over generations by the Hani people — fill entire hillsides and catch the light at dawn and dusk. Add a mild climate for much of the year and Yunnan's tea and coffee culture, and it becomes one of the most approachable regions in Western China.

Best time to visit

Comfortable for most of the year, with spring and autumn ideal. The rice terraces are at their most photogenic when flooded, roughly late autumn through early spring.

Best for

Ethnic villages and old towns, gentle nature and walking, cultural diversity, and an easygoing pace for travelers who prefer mild altitude.

Good to know

Distances between highlights are manageable by private vehicle and short flights. Shangri-La sits at higher elevation, so allow a little time to adjust.

Itineraries

Suggested Route Ideas

Starting points we can shape around your dates, pace and interests. Each is private, with your own guide and vehicle.

Dali, Lijiang & Shangri-La — 8–10 days

The classic north Yunnan route: Bai culture and Erhai Lake at Dali, the Naxi old town of Lijiang, Tiger Leaping Gorge and the Tibetan plateau edge at Shangri-La. A steady climb from lakeshore to highland.

Yuanyang Rice Terraces & the South — 5–7 days

A focused journey to the Hani rice terraces of Yuanyang, timed for sunrise and sunset light, with village stays and time among the south-eastern landscapes that frame them.
