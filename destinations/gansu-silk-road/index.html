Painted caves, the desert frontier and the corridor that carried the Silk Road — a journey through the heart of China's trade-route history.

Overview

Gansu & the Silk Road at a Glance

A Silk Road China tour through Gansu follows the Hexi Corridor, the narrow band of oasis towns between desert and mountain that funnelled trade between China and Central Asia for centuries. Its great prize is Dunhuang, home to the Mogao Caves — a vast complex of Buddhist grottoes filled with murals and statues — alongside the Mingsha singing sand dunes and the spring-fed Crescent Lake at their foot.

West along the corridor stands Jiayuguan, the fort marking the western end of the Great Wall, where the wall meets the desert. To the east, the layered colours of the Zhangye Danxia rainbow landform stripe entire hillsides, and the provincial capital Lanzhou sits on the Yellow River as the natural gateway to the region.

South of Lanzhou the landscape turns Tibetan: Labrang Monastery at Xiahe is one of the major monasteries of Tibetan Buddhism, while the cliffside Maiji Shan grottoes add another chapter of Buddhist cave art. Gansu links the desert, the wall and the plateau in a single, history-rich route.

Best time to visit

May to October. Late spring and autumn are most comfortable; summer is hot in the desert but good for the dunes and clear skies in the corridor.

Best for

Silk Road history, Buddhist cave art at Mogao and Maiji Shan, desert landscapes, and the meeting point of the Great Wall and the plateau.

Getting around

High-speed rail links the main corridor towns, with private vehicles for the caves, dunes and the Tibetan south. We pace the long distances carefully.

Itineraries

Suggested Route Ideas

Starting points we can shape around your dates, pace and interests. Each is private, with your own guide and vehicle.

Hexi Corridor Classic — 8–10 days

The signature Silk Road route from Lanzhou through Zhangye's Danxia landform and Jiayuguan's Great Wall fort to Dunhuang, with time for the Mogao Caves, the Mingsha dunes and Crescent Lake.

Silk Road & the Tibetan South — 10–12 days

Combine the Hexi Corridor with the Tibetan side of Gansu — Labrang Monastery at Xiahe and the grasslands nearby — and the cliffside grottoes of Maiji Shan, for art, history and culture together.
