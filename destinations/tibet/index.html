Overview

Tibet at a Glance

A Tibet tour in China usually begins in Lhasa, the historic and spiritual heart of the Tibetan plateau. Days here are spent at the Potala Palace, the Jokhang Temple and the Barkhor, the pilgrim circuit where Tibetan Buddhism is part of everyday life. Lhasa sits at around 3,650 metres, so the first day or two are deliberately gentle to let you acclimatise before travelling further.

Beyond the city, the landscape opens out. A classic plateau drive crosses high passes to the turquoise Yamdrok Lake, one of Tibet's sacred lakes, while longer routes continue west toward Shigatse and, for those with time, the north side of Everest Base Camp with its view of the world's highest mountain. Throughout, you travel with Tibetan-speaking guides and a private driver who know the roads, the seasons and the altitude.

Independent travel to Tibet is not permitted for international visitors — every trip requires a Tibet Travel Permit arranged through a licensed operator. We handle that paperwork and the supporting logistics so you can focus on the journey itself.

Best time to visit

April to October, with May–June and September–October offering the most settled weather and clearest mountain views. Winter is cold but quiet, with strong light on the plateau.

Best for

Tibetan Buddhist culture, sacred sites and monasteries, and high-altitude scenery — from Lhasa's temples to lakes, passes and the Himalayan frontier.

Good to know

A Tibet Travel Permit is required and must be arranged for you; we take care of it. Plan for altitude with an easy first day or two, and allow time to acclimatise before higher drives.

Itineraries

Suggested Route Ideas

Starting points we can shape around your dates, pace and interests. Each is private, with your own guide and vehicle.

Classic Lhasa & the Plateau — 8–10 days

Time in Lhasa for the Potala Palace, Jokhang Temple and the Barkhor, with day trips to Drepung and Sera monasteries. From there a plateau drive over the Kamba La pass to Yamdrok Lake, with built-in days for gentle acclimatisation.

Lhasa to Everest Base Camp — 12–14 days

The full overland route from Lhasa through Gyantse and Shigatse to the north side of Everest Base Camp, returning with stops at sacred lakes and monasteries. A longer, scenic journey for travelers comfortable with high altitude.
