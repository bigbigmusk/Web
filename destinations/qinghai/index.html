Overview

Qinghai at a Glance

Qinghai sits on the north-eastern Tibetan plateau and offers some of the most open, uncrowded scenery in Western China. Trips usually begin in the provincial capital, Xining, an easygoing city and the historic starting point of the Qinghai–Tibet railway. Just outside the city stands Kumbum (Ta'er) Monastery, one of the most important monasteries of Tibetan Buddhism.

The centrepiece is Qinghai Lake, the largest lake in China — a vast sheet of blue ringed by grasslands and, in summer, fields of yellow rapeseed in bloom. Roads circle the shore and run on toward the dazzling white flats of Chaka Salt Lake, where the still surface mirrors the sky.

Beyond the lakes lie the Tibetan grasslands and, deeper into the province, the high source region of several of Asia's great rivers. Distances are long and the land is sparsely populated, which makes Qinghai one of the finest road-trip destinations in the country for travelers who enjoy big landscapes and a slow pace.

Best time to visit

June to September, when the plateau is at its mildest. Mid to late summer brings the rapeseed bloom around Qinghai Lake, the most colourful time of year.

Best for

Lakes and grasslands, wide plateau scenery, Tibetan Buddhist culture, and relaxed road trips far from the crowds.

Getting around

Best explored by private vehicle on long, scenic loops from Xining. The plateau sits at altitude, so itineraries allow time to acclimatise.
